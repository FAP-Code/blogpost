The previous posts covered why Ghana's shift from cocoa to gold isn't real diversification, and how the same governance failure shows up across cashew, shea, rubber, horticulture, and oil palm. This post covers what *Beyond Cocoa* actually recommends: eight reforms, each naming a responsible institution and a target timeframe, rather than a general call for more investment.

## Five crop-specific reforms

**R1. Cashew: mandate domestic processing floors.** The TCDA should require a minimum of 30% of raw cashew production be processed domestically before export, rising to 60% by 2030, enforced through an export permit system, and paired with subsidised industrial energy tariffs for licensed processors. *Responsible: TCDA, Ministry of Energy, Ghana Revenue Authority. Target: Q4 2026.*

**R2. Shea: enforce the export ban and protect women processors.** The existing ban on raw shea exports during the processing season needs real enforcement, backed by a Shea Nut Price Stabilisation Fund (modelled on the Cocoa Stabilisation Mechanism) to shield women processors from the price volatility that gutted output in 2024. *Responsible: TCDA, Development Bank Ghana, EPA Carbon Markets Office. Target: enforcement Q2 2026, fund operational Q4 2026.*

**R3. Rubber: commission the export trial.** The industry's own proposal, a 3 to 6 month trial suspension of raw rubber exports to test domestic processing capacity, should actually be run, monitored by the TCDA and Ghana Statistical Service, with results feeding a permanent feedstock allocation framework. *Responsible: TCDA, Ghana Statistical Service, Energy Commission. Target: trial starting Q3 2026.*

**R4. Horticulture: cold chain first, then market access.** At least 8% of the annual road fund should be ring-fenced for feeder roads in the Eastern Region pineapple corridor and Northern Region mango zones, paired with public-private cold chain infrastructure and a GEPA-run fund to co-finance smallholder GlobalGAP certification. *Responsible: Ministry of Roads, GEPA, Ministry of Agriculture. Target: PPP structures tendered Q1 2027.*

**R5. Oil palm: link certification to smallholder clusters.** RSPO certification of smallholder oil palm clusters should be accelerated as a precondition for linking them to processing facilities, alongside preferential AfCFTA tariffs for certified Ghanaian exporters and district-level productivity extension to close the gap with estate yields. *Responsible: TCDA, Ministry of Trade, MoFA. Target: cluster certification programme launched Q2 2026.*

## Three reforms that cut across every crop

**R6. Establish an Agricultural Value Chain Coordination Office.** This is the recommendation the paper calls the single most important one: a body inside the Office of the President with binding authority over MoFA, MOTI, GEPA, TCDA, and the Development Bank Ghana, holding each accountable to quarterly performance targets. Without it, the institutional fragmentation described in the earlier posts persists no matter how well any one agency performs. *Responsible: Office of the President, Parliament. Target: legislation tabled Q1 2027.*

**R7. Redirect the Development Bank Ghana toward agro-processing.** The DBG should formally commit at least 30% of annual lending to rural agro-processing, on 7 to 15 year concessional terms, with a blended finance facility (combining DBG capital with IFC, AfDB, and bilateral concessional funding) targeting the $1 to 10 million range that both commercial banks and large development finance institutions currently ignore. *Responsible: DBG Board, Ministry of Finance. Target: portfolio mandate Q3 2026, blended facility Q1 2027.*

**R8. Create a Non-Traditional Agricultural Export Processing Fund.** Capitalised by a 3% levy on raw commodity exports across all TCDA priority sectors, ring-fenced and independently governed, spent only on processing infrastructure, energy subsidies for licensed processors, and smallholder certification support. It creates a direct fiscal loop: continued raw exports fund the reforms needed to eventually end raw exports. *Responsible: Ministry of Finance, TCDA. Target: fund design Q3 2026, first capitalisation Q4 2026.*

## Why this list, and not a bigger investment number

The paper is explicit that none of these are technically complex. What makes them hard is that they redistribute the processing margin away from foreign buyers and domestic export licence holders who currently benefit from the raw-export status quo, toward Ghanaian farmers, workers, and processors. The TCDA's $12 billion 2030 projection is a horizon, not a guarantee. Whether Ghana reaches it depends on whether these eight items get implemented, not just announced.
