Ghana's economic diversification conversation almost always turns into a question of money: more investment, more subsidies, more credit for farmers. That framing misses the actual bottleneck.

Across non-traditional agricultural exports (cashew, shea, rubber, oil palm, mango, and pineapple), the constraint is institutional coordination, not capital. Regulatory responsibility is split across agencies with overlapping mandates, and the gap between policy design and implementation is where value gets lost.

This is the starting argument of *Beyond Cocoa: Unlocking Ghana's Non-Traditional Agricultural Value Chains for Diversification and Inclusive Growth*. The full paper is in `research/beyond-cocoa/` in this repo.

More posts will unpack individual value chains, the comparative country case studies (South Korea, Singapore, Finland, and others), and what a realistic transformation timeline for Ghana looks like.
